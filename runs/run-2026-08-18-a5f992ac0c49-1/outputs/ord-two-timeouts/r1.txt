You use two timeouts because an LLM API call can fail in two very different ways: the provider may be unreachable, or it may be reachable but still generating slowly. A short connect timeout lets you fail fast on outages, while a longer overall timeout gives a real answer time to finish [llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts].

With streaming, the second timeout can also work differently: instead of bounding the whole call, it bounds the idle gap between chunks, so a slow but visibly progressing response stays open rather than failing and becoming an extra paid retry [llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts], [llm-cost-at-campus-scale--streaming-and-retries].
