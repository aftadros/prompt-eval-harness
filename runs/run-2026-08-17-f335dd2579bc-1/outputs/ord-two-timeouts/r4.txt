Because an LLM call has two very different failure modes: the provider may be unreachable, so you want to fail quickly; or it may be reachable but still generating a valid answer, where waiting longer is reasonable [llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts].

In practice, that means using one timeout as a fast “outage tripwire” for the connection attempt—`connect_timeout` in the example—and another as the overall ceiling for waiting on generation to finish—`timeout` for a non-streaming call [llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts]. With streaming, the second kind of limit is adjusted because the connection stays open while tokens arrive, so you typically bound idle gaps between chunks rather than the whole call [llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts].
