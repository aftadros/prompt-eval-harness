You set two timeouts because an LLM call has two very different failure modes: the provider may be unreachable, or it may be reachable but slow to finish generating [llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts]. A short connect timeout—such as 2 seconds—is a fast outage tripwire for when you can’t even reach the provider. A longer total timeout—such as 30 seconds for a non-streaming call—gives a reachable model enough time to produce a real answer [llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts].

With streaming, the timeout math changes: because the connection stays open while tokens arrive, you bound the idle gap between chunks instead of the whole call. That helps keep a slow but visibly progressing answer from failing and becoming an extra paid retry [llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts][llm-cost-at-campus-scale--streaming-and-retries].
