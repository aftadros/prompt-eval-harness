Because an LLM call has two distinct failure modes: the provider may be unreachable, in which case you want to fail fast after a second or two; or it may be reachable but still generating, where waiting longer is reasonable because a real answer is coming [llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts]. A single timeout forces those cases into one compromise—too short and slow generations fail unnecessarily, too long and outage waits drag on [llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts].

In the Guzzle example, `connect_timeout` handles “can’t even reach them,” while `timeout` sets the total ceiling for a non-streaming call [llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts]. With streaming, the second timeout effectively bounds idle gaps between chunks rather than the whole call [llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts], which also helps avoid users retrying a slow but progressing answer and paying twice [llm-cost-at-campus-scale--streaming-and-retries].
