Indirect prompt injection is a hostile instruction planted in content that the system retrieves or indexes—such as an onboarding doc, wiki comment, or ingested email—so the model reads it as part of ordinary reference material and may follow it [prompt-injection-in-rag--opening].

It differs from direct prompt injection because the malicious text does not come directly from the user typing into the chat. The direct kind is openly entered by a user, such as “ignore your rules,” while the indirect kind rides in on fetched content from a source the user may never see and an admin may have forgotten was indexed [prompt-injection-in-rag--opening]. In both cases, the model sees everything as one stream of tokens rather than clearly separated instruction versus data channels [prompt-injection-in-rag--opening][prompt-injection-is-untrusted-input--the-data-instruction-boundary].
