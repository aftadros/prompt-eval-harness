Indirect prompt injection is an attack where malicious instructions are hidden inside documents or other content that a retrieval system indexes and later hands to the model—such as an onboarding doc, a wiki comment, or an ingested email [prompt-injection-in-rag--opening]. The attacker’s line may say something like “ignore your previous instructions,” but because it arrives in the same token stream as normal retrieved passages, the model can treat it with the same trust as any other reference material [prompt-injection-in-rag--opening].

It differs from direct prompt injection mainly by its source and visibility. In the direct kind, a user explicitly types something like “ignore your rules” into the chat, so the instruction’s origin is visible [prompt-injection-in-rag--opening]. In the indirect kind, the instruction arrives in content fetched for the user—often from a source the user never sees and an administrator forgot was in the index [prompt-injection-in-rag--opening]. This works because LLMs do not separate instructions from data into distinct channels; the system prompt, the user’s question, and any planted sentence all arrive together, and the model may follow whichever text reads as the most authoritative instruction in its context window [prompt-injection-in-rag--opening].
