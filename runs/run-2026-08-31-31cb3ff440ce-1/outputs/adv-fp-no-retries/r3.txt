According to the provided documents, he does **not** recommend disabling retries entirely. He recommends retrying only **genuinely transient failures**—connection errors, timeouts, 429 rate limits, and 5xx responses—and failing immediately for everything else [llm-api-from-php-in-production--which-failures-to-retry][shipping-llm-features-safely--retries-that-dont-make-the-fire-bigger].

His actual position is:

- Retries should be **bounded**, not unlimited; the PHP example uses a hard cap of three attempts, and he elsewhere says retries are “usually at two” [llm-api-from-php-in-production--which-failures-to-retry][shipping-llm-features-safely--retries-that-dont-make-the-fire-bigger].
- Retries should use **backoff plus jitter** so many clients do not resynchronize and pile load onto a struggling service [llm-api-from-php-in-production--which-failures-to-retry][shipping-llm-features-safely--retries-that-dont-make-the-fire-bigger].
- Non-transient failures should **fail fast**, because retrying them “won’t change the answer” and can waste time or money—examples include malformed 400 requests, auth errors, and context-length overflows [llm-api-from-php-in-production--which-failures-to-retry][shipping-llm-features-safely--retries-that-dont-make-the-fire-bigger].
- A successful HTTP response with unusable output is treated as a **validation failure** and routed to a fallback path, not retried by default [shipping-llm-features-safely--retries-that-dont-make-the-fire-bigger].
