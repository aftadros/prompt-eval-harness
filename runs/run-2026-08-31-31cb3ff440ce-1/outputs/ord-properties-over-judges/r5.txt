Use a property check whenever the question lets you define a concrete, observable condition that an answer either has or doesn’t have — for example: it includes a cited source, is valid JSON, contains a fact you know belongs there, or refuses when refusing was the right call [did-the-prompt-change-help--build-the-set-from-real-questions-including-the].

Prefer property checks because they are cheaper to write than a model-graded rubric, far cheaper to run, and don’t drift between runs. So use them anywhere the question allows, and save model grading for answers that need real judgment [did-the-prompt-change-help--build-the-set-from-real-questions-including-the].

Use a model grader instead when you’re measuring qualities with no exact string match — such as whether an answer stayed grounded in its cited source, whether the tone fit the audience, or whether a refusal was polite and specific [evals-before-vibes--llm-as-judge-and-where-it-lies-to-you].
