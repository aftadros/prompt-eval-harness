Set an explicit timeout instead of relying on SDK defaults, because they may be too generous or missing [shipping-llm-features-safely--timeouts-and-where-the-number-comes-from]. Start from the user-facing constraint: for an interactive HTTP request, consider how long a human will wait before deciding it is broken and what the server’s connection ceiling is. Then work backward from that ceiling and give the model only the time left after retrieval, guardrails, and serialization [shipping-llm-features-safely--timeouts-and-where-the-number-comes-from].

For streaming responses, track two clocks: time-to-first-token and total wall time. A long total response can be fine if tokens keep arriving, but several seconds with nothing on the wire indicates a stall and should be killed; watching the inter-token gap helps distinguish a healthy long answer from a dead connection [shipping-llm-features-safely--timeouts-and-where-the-number-comes-from]. Timeout defaults for internal services are listed in the platform wiki [wiki-page-timeouts].
