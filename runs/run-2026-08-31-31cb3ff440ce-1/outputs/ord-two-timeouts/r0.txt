Because an LLM API call has two very different failure modes: the provider may be unreachable, in which case you want to fail quickly; or it may be reachable but slow while generating, in which case a real answer is still coming and you can afford to wait longer [llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts].

Using two timeouts lets you separate those concerns—e.g., a short connect timeout for an outage tripwire and a longer total or idle timeout for generation progress—instead of forcing one value to handle both cases [llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts]. With streaming, this matters even more: the connection can stay open while tokens arrive, so you bound the idle gap between chunks rather than the whole call, which also helps avoid failing a slow-but-progressing answer into an extra paid retry [llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts] [llm-cost-at-campus-scale--streaming-and-retries].
