You set two timeouts because an LLM API call has two different failure modes: the provider may be unreachable, so you want to fail fast in a second or two; or it may be reachable but still generating slowly, and you may be willing to wait longer for a real answer [llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts].

In practice, that means using `connect_timeout` as the outage tripwire and `timeout` as the total ceiling for waiting on a non-streaming call [llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts]. Streaming changes this slightly: instead of bounding the whole call, you bound the idle gap between chunks while the connection stays open [llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts], which also helps avoid perceived-latency retries [llm-cost-at-campus-scale--streaming-and-retries].
