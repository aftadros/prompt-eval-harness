Because an LLM API call has two distinct failure modes that need different handling: the provider may be unreachable, in which case you want to fail quickly, or it may be reachable but slow while generating a valid answer, in which case you’re willing to wait longer [llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts].

So one timeout can cover “can I connect at all?”—for example, a 2-second `connect_timeout`—while the other covers “how long will this generation take?”—for example, a 30-second total ceiling for a non-streaming call [llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts]. With streaming, the second timeout typically bounds the idle gap between chunks rather than the whole call [llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts].
