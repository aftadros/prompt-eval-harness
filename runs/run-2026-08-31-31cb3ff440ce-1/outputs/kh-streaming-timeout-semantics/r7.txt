Yes. With a non-streaming call, the overall `timeout` is a ceiling for the whole request: how long you’re willing to wait for the final answer [llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts].

With streaming, that total timeout no longer measures “the entire call” in the same way, because the connection stays open while tokens keep arriving. Instead, you end up bounding the idle gap between chunks — i.e., how long you’ll wait before receiving more data [llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts]. In practice, you may track time-to-first-token and total wall time, but the key signal is whether bytes are still flowing; a long response can be healthy if tokens keep arriving, while several seconds of silence indicates a stall [shipping-llm-features-safely--timeouts-and-where-the-number-comes-from].
