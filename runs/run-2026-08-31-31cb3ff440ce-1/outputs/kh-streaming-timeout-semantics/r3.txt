Yes. With a non-streaming call, the timeout can bound the whole request from start to finish [llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts]. With streaming, the connection stays open while tokens arrive, so the total timeout no longer measures “the entire answer” in the same way; instead you’re effectively bounding idle gaps between chunks or stalls on the wire [llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts] [shipping-llm-features-safely--timeouts-and-where-the-number-comes-from].

In practice, streaming means tracking multiple clocks: time-to-first-token and total wall time, plus inter-token gaps to distinguish a long healthy response from a dead connection [shipping-llm-features-safely--timeouts-and-where-the-number-comes-from].
