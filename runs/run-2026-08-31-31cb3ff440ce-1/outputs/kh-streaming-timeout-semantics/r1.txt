Yes. With a non-streaming call, the timeout is effectively a total ceiling on how long you wait for the full answer to finish generating [llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts]. With streaming, that meaning changes: the connection stays open while tokens arrive, so the useful timeout becomes the idle gap between chunks rather than the whole call alone [llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts].

In practice, a streamed response can run long in total and still be healthy if bytes keep arriving; what matters is detecting a stall when nothing arrives for several seconds [shipping-llm-features-safely--timeouts-and-where-the-number-comes-from]. So you typically track time-to-first-token and the inter-token/idle gap, rather than relying on total wall-clock time alone [shipping-llm-features-safely--timeouts-and-where-the-number-comes-from].
