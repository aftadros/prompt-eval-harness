No—not always, but it depends on the cost of being wrong. The documents describe refusal as a “precision/recall dial”: refusing too often means shrugging off questions the assistant could have answered, while refusing too little lets it produce confident hallucinations. For documentation people use to make decisions, they recommend leaning toward refusal when unsure, because a missed answer only costs someone a trip to a human, whereas a confident wrong answer can be acted on and discovered later [rag-over-internal-docs--the-honest-i-dont-know].
