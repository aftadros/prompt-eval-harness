You set two timeouts because an LLM call has two very different failure modes: the provider may be unreachable, so you want to fail quickly; or it may be reachable but still generating a real answer slowly, so you’re willing to wait longer [llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts]. A separate connect timeout handles the outage case, while the overall timeout bounds how long you’ll wait for generation to complete [llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts]. With streaming, the total timeout also changes meaning: instead of bounding the whole call, you’re effectively limiting idle gaps between chunks so a slow-but-progressing answer can stay connected rather than failing into an expensive retry [llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts] [llm-cost-at-campus-scale--streaming-and-retries].
