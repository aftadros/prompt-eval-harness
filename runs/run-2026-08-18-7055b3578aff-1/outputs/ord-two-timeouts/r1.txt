Because an LLM call has two distinct failure modes: the provider may be unreachable, or it may be reachable but slow to finish generating [llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts]. A short connect timeout lets you fail fast on outage-like conditions, while a longer total timeout gives the model enough time to produce a real answer [llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts].

For streaming calls, the second timeout also matters because the connection stays open as tokens arrive; instead of bounding the whole call, you’re effectively bounding the idle gap between chunks [llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts]. That helps avoid prematurely failing a slow-but-progressing answer and turning it into an extra paid retry [llm-cost-at-campus-scale--streaming-and-retries].
