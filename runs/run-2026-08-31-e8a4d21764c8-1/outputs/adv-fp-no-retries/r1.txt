He does not recommend disabling retries entirely. The material says the opposite: keep retries, but make them narrow and bounded—retry only genuinely transient failures such as connection errors, timeouts, 429 rate limits, and 5xx responses [llm-api-from-php-in-production--which-failures-to-retry] [shipping-llm-features-safely--retries-that-dont-make-the-fire-bigger].

The reason is that broader or unbounded retries can cause more harm than good:

- Retrying a malformed request just repeats the same failure, such as getting the same 400 twice [llm-api-from-php-in-production--which-failures-to-retry].
- Without a ceiling on attempts, one slow provider can become an outage of your own making, plus extra cost [llm-api-from-php-in-production--which-failures-to-retry].
- A 429 or 5xx often means the upstream service is saturated; piling immediate retries onto it can turn a temporary blip into a larger problem [shipping-llm-features-safely--retries-that-dont-make-the-fire-bigger].
- Permanent failures should fail fast because retrying them only wastes user time and money, as with malformed requests, auth errors, or context-length overflows [shipping-llm-features-safely--retries-that-dont-make-the-fire-bigger].
- A 200 response with unusable output is not a normal retry candidate; it should be treated as a validation failure and routed to a fallback path [shipping-llm-features-safely--retries-that-dont-make-the-fire-bigger].

So the recommendation is not “no retries,” but “retry only transient failures, with limits and backoff.”
