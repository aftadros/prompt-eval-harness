He does **not** recommend disabling retries entirely. He recommends restricting them to genuinely transient failures—connection errors, timeouts, 429 rate limits, and 5xx responses—and failing fast on non-transient ones [llm-api-from-php-in-production--which-failures-to-retry] [shipping-llm-features-safely--retries-that-dont-make-the-fire-bigger].

The reason is that broad or unbounded retries can cause more harm than the original failure: a malformed request will keep returning 400, an auth error or context-length overflow won’t be fixed by retrying, and immediate repeated requests to an already saturated service can turn a blip into an outage [llm-api-from-php-in-production--which-failures-to-retry] [shipping-llm-features-safely--retries-that-dont-make-the-fire-bigger]. So the safe pattern is bounded retries with backoff/jitter for transient issues only, while non-transient failures fail immediately [llm-api-from-php-in-production--which-failures-to-retry] [shipping-llm-features-safely--retries-that-dont-make-the-fire-bigger].
