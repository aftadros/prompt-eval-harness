The documents do not say he recommends disabling retries entirely. They establish a narrower policy: retry only genuinely transient failures—such as connection errors, timeouts, 429s, and 5xx responses—and keep those retries bounded with backoff and jitter [llm-api-from-php-in-production--which-failures-to-retry] [shipping-llm-features-safely--retries-that-dont-make-the-fire-bigger].

He avoids broader retrying because it can cause real damage: retrying a malformed 400 request, auth error, or context-length overflow won’t change the answer and wastes time and money [llm-api-from-php-in-production--which-failures-to-retry] [shipping-llm-features-safely--retries-that-dont-make-the-fire-bigger]. Retrying immediately against an already saturated upstream can also turn a brief problem into a larger outage [shipping-llm-features-safely--retries-that-dont-make-the-fire-bigger]. For responses that succeed with HTTP 200 but produce unusable output, the recommendation is not to retry by default but to route to a fallback path [shipping-llm-features-safely--retries-that-dont-make-the-fire-bigger].
