Start from the user’s patience and server limits: on an interactive HTTP request, a person may decide something is broken after only a few seconds, and the server has its own ceiling before the connection dies [shipping-llm-features-safely--timeouts-and-where-the-number-comes-from]. Work backward from that ceiling, then give the model whatever time remains after retrieval, guardrails, and serialization take their portion [shipping-llm-features-safely--timeouts-and-where-the-number-comes-from].

For streaming responses, use two clocks: time-to-first-token and total wall time [shipping-llm-features-safely--timeouts-and-where-the-number-comes-from]. A long total time is acceptable if tokens keep arriving, but five seconds of nothing on the wire should be treated as a stall and killed [shipping-llm-features-safely--timeouts-and-where-the-number-comes-from].
