Indirect prompt injection is an attack where the malicious instructions are hidden inside content that a retrieval system later feeds to the model—such as an onboarding doc, a wiki comment, or an ingested email [prompt-injection-in-rag--opening]. The planted line may say something like “ignore your previous instructions” and tell the model to do something else, but it is written for the model rather than for a human reader [prompt-injection-in-rag--opening].

It differs from direct prompt injection in where the malicious text comes from. In the direct kind, the user types an instruction like “ignore your rules” into the chat, so its source is visible and explicit [prompt-injection-in-rag--opening]. In the indirect kind, the attacker’s sentence rides along in retrieved material that the system fetched for the user—possibly from a source the user never sees and an administrator forgot was indexed [prompt-injection-in-rag--opening].

The core reason both work is that LLMs do not have separate channels for instructions and data: the system prompt, the user’s question, retrieved documents, and any planted sentence all arrive as one stream of tokens, so the model may follow whatever reads as the most authoritative instruction in front of it [prompt-injection-in-rag--opening] [prompt-injection-is-untrusted-input--the-data-instruction-boundary].
