None of the techniques described fully solves prompt injection. The documents explicitly say LLMs do not provide a prepared-statement-style separation between instructions and data, so marking boundaries or escaping output only lowers risk rather than guaranteeing immunity [prompt-injection-is-untrusted-input--the-data-instruction-boundary]. The stated approach is defense in depth: delimiters, provenance checks, least-privilege tools, output escaping, logging, and guardrails are layered because “no single one has to hold” and no layer can be trusted with the whole job [prompt-injection-is-untrusted-input--defense-in-depth]. In RAG specifically, structural marking of retrieved context “lowers the success rate without being a guarantee,” and the goal is narrower—preventing one planted instruction from being fatal rather than making arbitrary indexed text safe [prompt-injection-in-rag--the-prompt-and-the-output].
