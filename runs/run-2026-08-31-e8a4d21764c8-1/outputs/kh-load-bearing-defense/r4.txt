The single most important defense is **enforcing access permissions at retrieval time**: filter candidate documents against the user’s rights before anything is ranked, assembled into a prompt, or seen by the model [prompt-injection-in-rag--push-the-defense-earlier-than-the-prompt]. That permission filter is explicitly described as “the load-bearing piece,” because even if an injected instruction succeeds at the model level, it still cannot reach data the user was never cleared for [prompt-injection-in-rag--the-prompt-and-the-output].

Everything else—marking retrieved context as untrusted reference material, screening attack-like text, escaping outputs, and limiting tools—is useful, but mainly increases the attacker’s cost rather than providing the core protection [prompt-injection-in-rag--push-the-defense-earlier-than-the-prompt] [prompt-injection-in-rag--the-prompt-and-the-output].
