Because an LLM API call has two very different failure modes, and one timeout would force you to treat them the same way.

- **Connect timeout**: guards against the provider being unreachable; it lets you “fail fast” in a second or two and move on [llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts].
- **Generation/total timeout**: covers the case where the provider is reachable but slow to finish generating, so you can wait longer for a real answer [llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts].

For streaming calls, the second timeout also changes meaning: instead of bounding the whole call, it can bound the idle gap between chunks. That matters because an answer that is slow but visibly making progress can keep its connection open rather than failing and turning into a retry you pay for [llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts] [llm-cost-at-campus-scale--streaming-and-retries].
