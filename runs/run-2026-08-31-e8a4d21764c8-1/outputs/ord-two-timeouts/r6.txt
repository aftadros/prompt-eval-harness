You set two timeouts because an LLM API call has two different failure modes: the provider may be unreachable, or it may be reachable but slow to finish generating a real answer [rag-over-internal-docs--llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts]. A short connect timeout lets you fail fast during an outage in about a second or two, while a longer total timeout lets you wait for generation to complete when the provider is actually working [rag-over-internal-docs--llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts].

In Guzzle terms, that’s why you can use something like `connect_timeout => 2` and `timeout => 30`: the first handles “can’t reach them,” while the second bounds how long you’re willing to wait for an answer [rag-over-internal-docs--llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts]. With streaming, the total timeout can also shift from covering the whole call to bounding the idle gap between chunks, since the connection stays open while tokens arrive [rag-over-internal-docs--llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts]. That matters because a slow answer that is visibly progressing can stay open instead of failing and turning into an extra paid retry [rag-over-internal-docs--llm-cost-at-campus-scale--streaming-and-retries].
