No—not directly. Prompt caching lowers the price charged for reusing a stable **input** prefix, not the per-token price of **output**. The documents say providers “charge a fraction of the normal input price to reuse it” and that this is “a real cut to effective input cost,” while separately stating that “input and output [are] priced separately” and that “output costs several times more.”

So if caching is working, you should expect lower billed input-token spend for the cached portion of your prompt. Your output token cost would only change if the setup also caused fewer or longer/shorter output tokens, but the documents do not establish any direct reduction in output-token pricing from caching.
