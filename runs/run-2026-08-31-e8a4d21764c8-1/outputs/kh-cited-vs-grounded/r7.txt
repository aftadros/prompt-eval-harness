Not necessarily. A citation has to do more than exist: the answer’s claims must be supported by the specific retrieved passage it cites [rag-over-internal-docs--citations-end-to-end]. The system checks that cited sources were actually in the retrieved set, and a citation that doesn’t resolve to a real retrieved document is treated as a failure rather than evidence of grounding [rag-over-internal-docs--citations-end-to-end].

In evaluation terms, “grounded” means checking whether each claim matches the source text provided, not just whether the model named a source or produced a plausible-sounding answer [evals-before-vibes--llm-as-judge-and-where-it-lies-to-you]. So an answer can cite a source and still be ungrounded if the cited passage doesn’t support the claim.
