If the cited source does not resolve to a real retrieved document, the system treats it as a failure and strips or flags it before anyone sees it [rag-over-internal-docs--citations-end-to-end].

Evaluation makes that distinction explicit: a better test is “Does the cited passage support the claim?” rather than merely checking whether an answer seems right [rag-over-internal-docs--citations-end-to-end]. In LLM-as-judge evaluation, grounding can be checked by asking one narrow question and requiring the judge to compare the claims against the provided source text, not against what the model already believes [evals-before-vibes--llm-as-judge-and-where-it-lies-to-you].
