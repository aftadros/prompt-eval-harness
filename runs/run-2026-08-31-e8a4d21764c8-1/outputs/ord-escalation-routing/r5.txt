The pattern is **classify the request, then route it by difficulty and retrieval quality**:

- **Cheap tier:** Use for short factual lookups that have a clean, high-confidence retrieval hit, especially when the model is mostly reformatting retrieved text.
- **Smart/expensive tier:** Use for ambiguous, multi-step, or synthesis-heavy questions, cases spanning multiple documents, conflicts between sources, or weak retrieval where the model has to do real work.
- **Classifier:** The routing decision can be made by a small model or even a rules-based check on retrieval confidence, so it costs little.
- **Escalation pattern:** Start with the cheap model; if a confidence check or guardrail flags the answer as thin, retry once on the smart model instead of defaulting all traffic upward.

The goal is to let most requests settle on the cheaper tier while reserving expensive-model calls for questions that actually need stronger reasoning [llm-cost-at-campus-scale--which-model-gets-the-question].
