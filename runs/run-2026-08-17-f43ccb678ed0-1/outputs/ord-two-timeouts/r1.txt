Because an LLM call has two different failure modes: the provider may be unreachable, in which case you want to fail quickly after a short connect timeout, or it may be reachable but slow while generating, so you may want to allow a longer overall timeout for a real answer to finish [llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts].

A single timeout would force those two situations to share one limit. With separate timeouts, the short value acts as an outage tripwire, while the longer value bounds how long you wait for generation [llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts]. For streaming calls, the total-timeout math changes: since tokens keep arriving over an open connection, you may instead bound the idle gap between chunks rather than the whole call [llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts] [llm-cost-at-campus-scale--streaming-and-retries].
