You set two timeouts because an LLM call has two different failure modes: the provider may be unreachable, or it may be reachable but slow to generate [llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts]. A short connect timeout lets you fail fast if the service is down, while a longer overall timeout lets you wait for a real answer that is still being generated [llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts].

With one timeout, you’d have to compromise: too short and slow generations may be killed unnecessarily; too long and an outage can make the request hang. In Guzzle terms, `connect_timeout` handles “can’t even reach them,” while `timeout` sets the total ceiling for a non-streaming call [llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts]. For streaming, the total timeout changes meaning because the connection stays open as tokens arrive, so you may instead bound the idle gap between chunks rather than the whole call [llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts][llm-cost-at-campus-scale--streaming-and-retries].
