Because a single timeout can’t distinguish two very different failure modes in an LLM API call: the provider may be unreachable, or it may be reachable but still generating a slow answer [llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts]. A short connect timeout lets you fail fast when you can’t reach the provider at all—on the order of one or two seconds—while a longer overall timeout gives a real generation time to finish [llm-api-from-php-in-production--two-timeouts].

With streaming, this matters even more: the connection stays open while tokens arrive, so instead of bounding the whole call you may need to bound the idle gap between chunks. Visible progress also reduces perceived latency and stops users from retrying a slow-but-working request, which would otherwise mean paying for multiple generations [llm-cost-at-campus-scale--streaming-and-retries].
